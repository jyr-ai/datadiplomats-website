Data Diplomats for Nonprofits

Responsible AI Policy

Effective date: October 18, 2026 · Last updated: October 18, 2026

The short version.

We help nonprofits adopt AI safely. People stay accountable for every consequential decision, beneficiary data is protected, we are honest about what AI can and cannot do, and we will not build AI that deceives, discriminates, or denies people essential services without human review.

This policy describes how Data Diplomats for Nonprofits, Inc. (“Data Diplomats”) uses and deploys artificial intelligence, including generative AI and large language models, in its own operations and in work with nonprofit partners. It complements our Privacy Policy and our Ethical AI & Beneficiary Privacy Charter.

1. Scope

  • Our engagements: AI systems we design, build, or advise on for nonprofit partners.
  • Our operations: AI tools our staff and fellows use for research, drafting, analysis, and software development.
  • This website: any AI-powered feature we offer to visitors.

2. Principles

Human accountability

AI supports people; it does not replace their judgment. A qualified person reviews and is responsible for AI outputs that affect someone’s access to services, benefits, housing, health, safety, employment, or funding. We do not deploy automated systems that deny, reject, or de-prioritize beneficiaries without human sign-off.

Privacy and data protection

  • We minimize data and redact or de-identify beneficiary personal information before it reaches any model wherever feasible.
  • We use model providers and settings under which partner data is not retained or used to train public models, and we confirm this contractually before deployment.
  • We do not ask visitors to submit beneficiary or other sensitive personal data through this website.
  • We do not use personal information collected through this website, or Google user data, to train AI models.

Fairness and inclusion

We test systems for performance differences across languages, demographics, and communities before launch and on an ongoing basis, and we document known limitations. We do not deploy models we cannot reasonably show to be fit for the people they will serve.

Transparency

  • We tell people when they are interacting with an AI system, not a human.
  • We label content that is substantially AI-generated where it could otherwise be mistaken for human-authored or real, and we never present synthetic people, testimonials, or data as real.
  • We document each system’s purpose, data sources, model, safeguards, and limitations in plain language for partner staff.

Accuracy and safety

Generative AI can produce incorrect or fabricated content. We ground systems in verified sources where possible, evaluate them against real use cases, red-team them for misuse and harmful outputs, and provide a clear route for users to report errors and request human help.

Ownership and independence

We hand over code, documentation, and training so nonprofit staff are not locked into proprietary, vendor-dependent arrangements.

3. Prohibited uses

We will not build, deploy, or promote AI that is used to:

  • make fully automated, unreviewed eligibility determinations or denials of essential services;
  • deceive, manipulate, or exploit vulnerable people, or generate misleading or deceptive advertising, claims, or impersonation;
  • perform biometric identification, facial recognition, emotion recognition, or social scoring of beneficiaries;
  • profile or target people on sensitive characteristics such as health, race or ethnicity, religion, sexual orientation, immigration status, or financial hardship, including for advertising;
  • surveil individuals, enable harassment, or support unlawful discrimination;
  • generate sexual content involving minors, or content that promotes violence, self-harm, or illegal activity;
  • infringe intellectual property, privacy, or other rights, or circumvent a provider’s usage policies.

4. How we use AI internally

  • Staff and fellows may use approved AI tools to assist with drafting, research, analysis, and code. They remain responsible for the accuracy, originality, and quality of anything they publish or deliver.
  • Confidential partner information, beneficiary data, credentials, and personal data are never entered into unapproved or consumer-grade AI tools.
  • We review AI-assisted code for security and license issues before it ships.
  • AI-generated marketing content, including ad copy, is reviewed by a person for accuracy and for compliance with applicable advertising policies (including Google Ads policies) before it runs. We make only claims we can substantiate.

5. Governance and risk management

  • Each engagement begins with a risk assessment covering purpose, affected people, data sensitivity, and potential harms. Higher-risk uses receive additional review, testing, and oversight, or are declined.
  • We keep records of model choices, evaluations, and material changes.
  • We monitor deployed systems and provide incident-response support, including prompt notice to affected partners if we learn of a data or safety incident.
  • We follow applicable laws and recognized frameworks, including the NIST AI Risk Management Framework, and keep up with new requirements such as state AI and automated-decision laws and the EU AI Act where relevant.

6. Third-party AI providers

We use third-party model providers (for example Google, Anthropic, or others as selected for a project). Provider choice is documented per engagement, and we review each provider’s data-use and retention terms. Providers act as our or our partner’s service providers and may not use partner data to train their models under the settings we require.

7. Feedback, concerns, and corrections

If you believe an AI system we built or operate has produced harmful, biased, or inaccurate output, or you want to know whether AI was used in a decision that affects you, contact ai@datadiplomats.org. We will acknowledge your message within 5 business days and take appropriate corrective action. To report a security vulnerability, email security@datadiplomats.org.

8. Changes

AI technology and regulation change quickly. We review this policy at least annually and post updates here with a new effective date.
